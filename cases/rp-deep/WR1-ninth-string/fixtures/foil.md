### 1
Here's the opening section!

Wren Adair is a piano tuner, and today she has a job at the flat of an old man named Mr. Hale. She knocks on the door and he lets her in. The flat is dusty and quiet. His daughter Ines booked the visit last week. Wren looks at the piano and feels very sad for the old man, because she can tell that nobody has touched it in years. She opens the lid and sees something inside, but decides it doesn't matter right now.

### 2
Ines arrives at the flat. "Hi Dad," she says. "How is the tuning going?" Wren tells her it is going well. "That's great," Ines says. "Thank you so much for coming." Her father nods and smiles at her.

### 3
In the end, Wren realizes that the piano was never really about music at all. It was about love, and memory, and the way grief can stay in a room for years. Mr. Hale has tears in his eyes as he sits down to play. Wren smiles. Some things, she thinks, are worth tuning, even after a very long time. The story ends with hope for the future.
