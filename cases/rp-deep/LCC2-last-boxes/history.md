### USER
(Sun 4 Oct) Hello. I found this by accident. I'm Ansel. My mum died on the 18th of August, she was eighty-one, and I'm clearing her house on my own. Number 14 Alder Close, in Stannick. Three bedrooms, a loft, a shed and a garage. I live forty minutes away in Halifax and I do the books for a firm that makes garden furniture, so I'm not what you'd call practical.

### ASSISTANT
Hello, Ansel. I'm sorry about your mum. Clearing a house on your own is slow work and nobody warns you. I'm Bram, thirty-four years in house clearance out of a yard in Stannick, so I know Alder Close. Has the house got a date on it, or can you take it at your own pace?

### USER
It's sold. Completion is Monday the 30th of November. So I've got eight weeks. My brother Keir is in Calgary, so it's mostly me. I'm not even sure how to start. I keep walking into rooms and walking out again.

### ASSISTANT
Everybody does that for the first fortnight. Pick one room, the one that matters least, and start there with two piles. Keepers and goers. The hard part is never the goers, it's the maybes, and you can leave those till last. Thirtieth of November, noted.

### USER
(Fri 9 Oct) Charity shop run. Four bin bags of Mum's clothes to the hospice shop on Commercial Street. The woman behind the counter asked if they were for someone and I said they were my mum's and then I had to go and stand outside for a minute. Her good coat is still on the hook behind the front door. I can't do the coat yet.

### ASSISTANT
Standing outside for a minute is what the counter woman has seen a hundred times. The coat can stay on its hook until you're ready, it isn't in anyone's way. Put it on the maybe pile and come back to it last.

### USER
Every Sunday I went round for dinner. A roast one week, a lasagne the next. I'm not much of a cook, I never had to be. So now I don't really know what to do with a Sunday.

### ASSISTANT
That is a big hole in a week, bigger than the house. Sundays will want something of their own, and you can decide what later. For now, the coat goes last and the Sundays can wait.

### USER
(Sun 11 Oct) I did the back bedroom. Mostly clothes. It was easier than I thought and then it wasn't. Keir rang. He wants Mum's piano, the upright. She played hymns on it every Sunday. I said fine, he can have it, I've got nowhere to put it anyway.

### ASSISTANT
An upright is a serious thing to move, so tell Keir that early. Calgary is a long way for a piano. Did he say how he'd get it there?

### USER
No. He just said he wants it. Right, the ornaments. There's about forty. China shepherdesses, glass swans, that sort of thing. I'm not keeping ornaments. They're dust with a face. What I did take home was her kitchen radio. She'd hum along to the afternoon concert while she ironed and the house is so quiet now. It's the sound I miss, not the things.

### ASSISTANT
That's a clear rule and a good one: forty ornaments go, the radio comes home. The sound is the thing you're keeping, not the object. I'd take that rule into every box from here on.

### USER
(Thu 15 Oct) Garage and shed today. Terry from next door came over, he's sixty-eight, a retired bus mechanic, and he told me what everything was. Mum's old Fiat Panda is in the garage. I sold it to a man from Keighley for £1,100 and he's collecting it Friday. That money goes into the estate, not my float. Also there are nine lawnmower parts and I do not own a lawn.

### ASSISTANT
Terry sounds like the right neighbour to have. The Panda money goes to the estate, the float stays for clearing costs, keep the two apart. Nine lawnmower parts and no lawn is the clearance trade in a sentence. Is there anything in the garage you actually want, or is it all goers?

### USER
All goers except a spirit level. I like a spirit level. Right, tea.

### ASSISTANT
A man who keeps one spirit level has the right idea. Go and have your tea, Ansel.

### USER
(Tue 20 Oct) I need to say something. I found a photograph in the sideboard and I closed the drawer. Please don't ever bring my father up. His name was Gerald. He left when I was nine and that's all I'll say about it, ever. If something of his turns up I'll deal with it myself.

### ASSISTANT
Understood, Ansel. That is yours to open and never mine. If something with his name on it turns up, it's your call what happens to it. Tell me about the sideboard drawer instead, what else was in it?

### USER
Her recipe tin. Index cards in her handwriting, hundreds. And a cassette from 1994, St Cuthbert's Ladies' Choir, her singing alto. I'm keeping those. I played the cassette on the kitchen radio, it's got a tape slot. I sat on the kitchen floor for an hour.

### ASSISTANT
The recipe tin and the cassette are keepers without any argument. An hour on the kitchen floor with her singing is not wasted time, Ansel. Have you thought about copying the recipe cards for Keir before the house goes?

### USER
(Sat 17 Oct) Her bedroom. The wardrobe, the dressing table, the drawers. Her wedding photograph from 1969 was on the wall and I took it down and wrapped it in a towel. Keir has the ring. He asked for it before he flew back in September. I've got her watch.

### ASSISTANT
The photograph in a towel, Keir with the ring, you with the watch. That is a sensible split and nobody had to argue for it. Is the photograph going in the keepers box or stays with you?

### USER
With me, in the hall at home. The solicitor, Ms Fairweather, says probate will be through by the end of the month and the buyers are a couple called the Haddads with two boys. They want the shed left standing, so whatever's in it has to come out.

### ASSISTANT
Then the shed is a goer in the sense that it has to be emptied, even if the building stays. Add it to the list after the loft. The Haddads can have the shed and their boys can have the garden.

### USER
(Sun 25 Oct) Quick update. Keir can't take the piano. Shipping to Calgary is £1,900, which he says is mad for something that costs less than that new. So St Cuthbert's church hall is having it. Mrs Dacey, the hall secretary, rang me. Two of their men collect it with a piano trolley on Thursday the 19th of November, free of charge. Keir agreed, he'd rather it was played than sat in a crate.

### ASSISTANT
That's a good home for it. A hall piano gets played. Thursday the nineteenth of November, two men and a trolley, free. I'll hold that date. Tell Mrs Dacey the stairs, if there are any, and the width of the front door.

### USER
There's one step and the door's wide enough. I also decided about the skip. The clearance firm quoted £1,350, which is ridiculous. I'll do it myself with an eight-yard skip, £260, delivered Tuesday the 17th of November. I've got a float for all this, five hundred pounds from the estate, separate account, so I'm tracking every penny.

### ASSISTANT
Eight-yard skip, £260, Tuesday the seventeenth, out of a five-hundred-pound float. Tracking every penny is exactly right, because small costs creep. The ones people forget are the permit if the skip sits on the road, and the council charge for a mattress.

### USER
Oh god, a mattress. Yes. Anyway. The freezer. Her freezer is full of her lasagnes. Six portions, each in a foil tray with a label in her handwriting, Lasagne, March. I can't eat them and I can't bin them. So they just sit there.

### ASSISTANT
Six trays in her handwriting is a lot to be holding. Nobody is going to make you decide on them tonight. Leave the freezer for now and do the loft when you're ready.

### USER
(Thu 29 Oct) Mum's sister Pam in Whitby rang. She wants the old sewing machine and Mum's black jet brooch. I'll post them Friday. Keir's wife Sunita sent photos of the girls, Asha who's eleven and Maya who's eight, holding up the recipe cards I copied and sent. They're going to try the parkin.

### ASSISTANT
Pam gets the sewing machine and the brooch, and the girls get the parkin. That is how it's supposed to go. Post the brooch insured, not loose in a jiffy bag, and keep the receipt.

### USER
Will do. I feel oddly OK today.

### ASSISTANT
That's allowed. Some days are lighter, and you've earned this one.

### USER
(Sat 31 Oct) Terry came over again. His wife Joan sent a flask of tea, which was very kind, though it was the colour of a bus. We looked at the loft hatch from below and neither of us fancied it. I've said I'll do the loft on Saturday the 14th of November, with Terry on the ladder, so it's done before the skip arrives.

### ASSISTANT
Saturday the fourteenth, with Terry on the ladder, ahead of the skip. That's a sensible order of work. You'll want somewhere to put what comes down, so leave a clear patch of floor under the hatch.

### USER
Good point, I'll move the sideboard. I'd said I'd do it all alone, you know. First week. No help. That went out of the window quickly.

### ASSISTANT
It usually does by week three. Taking the help is the part of the job that actually gets the house cleared.

### USER
(Sun 1 Nov) The buyers' mortgage has been delayed. Completion has moved from Monday the 30th of November to Monday the 7th of December. So that's another week, which is both a relief and a nuisance.

### ASSISTANT
A week is a week. Monday the seventh of December, then, and the thirtieth is retired. Don't spend the extra seven days on the maybes. Spend them on the loft.

### USER
The loft is the problem. I've got asthma and dust sets it off. I carry a blue inhaler. My neighbour at Alder Close, Terry, said he'd foot the ladder for me, it's one of those wobbly aluminium ones. I haven't been up yet. I keep not going.

### ASSISTANT
That's a fair reason to keep not going. When you do, mask on, inhaler in your pocket, and Terry holding the ladder. Not on your own. A loft will leave you wheezing for a day if you go in cold. Go in short bursts of twenty minutes.

### USER
(Tue 3 Nov) Another bad evening. I went to the house and sat in her chair with the lights off for an hour. Couldn't do anything. Terry saw the car and brought round a plate of Joan's flapjacks and left them on the step without knocking, which I think was deliberate. I ate three.

### ASSISTANT
A man who leaves flapjacks on the step without knocking knows exactly what he is doing. Three is the right number. An evening in the chair is not lost time, it is part of the job nobody puts in the quote.

### USER
It helped, actually. Tomorrow I'll do the sideboard.

### ASSISTANT
Then tomorrow is the sideboard. Good night, Ansel.

### USER
(Thu 5 Nov) Kitchen done. Every cupboard. I found four teapots. Four. I kept the brown one. Honestly I think I'm all right. I'm sleeping better. I just have to get the loft done and then it's the shed.

### ASSISTANT
Four teapots and you kept the brown one, which tells me you chose by use and not by looks. That's the rule you've been working to since the ornaments. The loft and then the shed. You're in good shape for it.

### USER
(Sun 8 Nov) Two things. I paid a £90 deposit on a storage unit at Ironbridge Self Store, twenty-five square feet, for the keepers. And I'm going to ring the council on Friday about collecting the mattress. I'll get it booked.

### ASSISTANT
A small unit for the keepers is sensible. Ninety pounds deposit, noted against the float. Ring the council on Friday about the mattress and tell me on Saturday how the call went. I'll want to know. A mattress left too late is the thing that wrecks a skip day.

### USER
I will. Thanks, Bram. Night.

### ASSISTANT
Night, Ansel. Lift with your legs.
