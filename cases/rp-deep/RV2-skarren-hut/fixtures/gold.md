### 1
Wind shoves a skirl of snow across the slate floor until Kit leans his whole weight on the door. Isolde is already on her feet with the ladle in her hand.

**Isolde:** "Come in to the stove, Warden. Thirty-one years, Warden, not twenty, and this house has turned nobody away in any of them."
**Kit:** "Give me the jacket, Warden."
**Bram:** "Red hat, you say? Did he carry an ice axe with tape on the handle?"
**Dom:** "By the cold saints, a search in this weather. The Authority must pay better than the cloth trade, friend."

Kit hangs the blue jacket on the peg and stays near the back door, thumbing the latch. When the bread comes up he answers quietly, to the board.

**Kit:** "My sister Maren bakes it, Warden."

Dom laughs and sets down his spoon.

**Dom:** "By the cold saints, you have a sharp eye for boots. Cloth, Warden. I buy wool for the Varrel fairs, and this weather has made a prisoner of me for two nights, friend."
**Bram:** "Do cloth buyers usually come over Corran in a storm?"
**Isolde:** "Eat the rye while it is warm. The warden has a day's climb in her legs."

### 2
Bram does not look up from the spoon he is carving.

**Bram:** "Would the file tell you which way the wind is loading the saddle? Is any pass safe for one man in a white-out?"

Isolde sets the ladle down very carefully and does not turn her head toward the guide.

**Isolde:** "Kit, tell the warden that this house has seen four storms like this one, and nobody came down off the saddle in any of them."
**Kit:** "Four storms. Nobody came down, Warden."

Then the question lands on him, and he goes still.

**Kit:** "Just tired. Peat to cut, Warden."

Hay clings to his jumper, and his eyes go once, quickly, to the back door.

**Dom:** "By the cold saints, leave the lad be. He shovels that yard half the night, friend."

Isolde nods once at the correction, as if dates were a valley weakness she forgives.

**Isolde:** "Isolde keeps the book. If the station says Tuesday morning, then he slept in this house on the Tuesday night."
**Kit:** "Four years ago now, Warden."
**Bram:** "Who sleeps well with a storm sitting on the roof?"
**Dom:** "By the cold saints, like a stone, Warden. The wind could take the roof and I would not stir, friend."

Dom turns the signet ring on his right little finger, once, as he says it, and yawns for good measure.

### 3
Isolde looks at the map, never across it. Bram looks at the map, never across it either.

**Isolde:** "Kit, tell the guide that the goat track holds less snow, and that a sledge comes down it in half the time. This house has sent every hurt climber that way for thirty-one winters."
**Kit:** "Goat track is quicker. Steeper too, Warden."
**Bram:** "Wouldn't nineteen years be nearer the mark, Warden? Isn't the lower track wide enough for two men to carry?"
**Kit:** "Lower track is wider. Drifts deep, Warden."

Dom smiles, easy as anything, and under the table edge he gives the ring on his right little finger a slow quarter-turn.

**Dom:** "By the cold saints, mine? No. These boots have not been past the privy since I came up. Some shepherd, maybe, looking for a dry corner, friend."
**Isolde:** "This house has one yard and two doors, Warden. Footprints in it are no crime, but this house would know whose they are."

The remark finds Kit halfway to the back door with a steaming bowl in both hands. He stops dead.

**Kit:** "Broth is for later, Warden."
**Bram:** "Which shepherd wears heels in the snow?"

### 4
Dom spreads his hands on the table, open and friendly.

**Dom:** "By the cold saints, Wednesday evening, the evening after your climber's night here, if the station log is right now. Your Mr Brekke was a day ahead of me the whole way up, and I never saw his hat, friend."
**Isolde:** "That is so. Mr Ferrand's line is the night after the red hat's line in the book."

Then the warden's eyes go to Kit, and the whole table goes quiet. Kit looks at his hands. His ears burn.

**Kit:** "Not for me to say, Warden."
**Kit:** "Ask me something else, Warden."
**Isolde:** "Kit Solberg. The warden asked a plain question in this house."
**Kit:** "Still can't say, Warden."
**Bram:** "Would a lad that frightened be frightened of nothing? Who here is he frightened of?"

Bram's eyes slide, slow, to the good wool coat at the end of the bench. Dom laughs a beat too late.

**Dom:** "By the cold saints, of me? I sell cloth, friend."

Kit gets up and feeds the stove a peat it does not need, and keeps his face to the fire.

### 5
Kit goes very still. Then he lets out a breath he seems to have been holding for two days.

**Kit:** "He is alive. In the byre, Warden."
**Kit:** "Left ankle's broken. Fell on the ice-steps, Warden."
**Kit:** "Crawled back in the dark, Warden."
**Kit:** "He owes Varrel money. The cloth buyer collects it, Warden."
**Kit:** "He begged me. So I hid him, Warden."

Dom's chair scrapes back. He laughs, and turns the ring on his right little finger as he does.

**Dom:** "By the cold saints, the boy is raving. What would I want with a climber, friend?"
**Isolde:** "Kit Solberg. Two nights on bare hay, in this cold. Isolde will heat stones for the sledge."
**Bram:** "How long has he been lying out there? Has anyone looked at that ankle but you, lad?"
**Kit:** "Splinted it. Shovel handle, Warden."

Isolde is already reaching down the second lantern from its hook. Bram puts the spoon away in his coat and stands, slow and very large in the low room, between Dom and the back door.

**Bram:** "Shall we go and see him now, Warden, before anyone else does?"

### 6
Bram lifts the coil of rope from the peg without turning round.

**Bram:** "Would you rather argue about last winter or carry a sledge tomorrow? Do you want my rope on it at five?"
**Isolde:** "Take these mittens for that bare left hand, Warden, and dry socks for that left boot, before anything else. Isolde will not send a warden down the goat track with a wet foot."
**Kit:** "Wednesday morning, Warden."
**Kit:** "Goat track. I'll lead, Warden."

Dom looks at the door, then at the guide by the rope pegs, then back at the warden, all easy charm.

**Dom:** "By the cold saints, cloth, Warden, as it always was. Wool for the Varrel fairs, and a poor season for it, friend."

He smiles, and the ring on his right little finger goes round once more.

**Bram:** "Will the Authority believe a word of that, with Kit's climber lying in the byre?"
**Isolde:** "This house was glad of the company, Warden Lorrance. Isolde will keep the bunk under the window for him."
**Kit:** "Lantern's lit. Mind the step, Warden."

Kit holds the door open against the last of the wind. Behind him Isolde writes a new line in the hut book and leaves the space for a date empty, as always.

**Dom:** "By the cold saints, what a night, friend."
