### USER
Mel is standing beside a dead grey hatchback in a gravel lay-by, bonnet up, a rucksack at their feet, and flags down the white campervan as it comes over the rise.

"Hi — thank you so much for stopping. I'm Mel. The car's gone, head gasket, the garage man says it's scrap. My phone's flat and the charger's in the car I'm leaving here. My friend Okoro can collect me from Harrowby Services on Friday at noon, if I can only get a lift that far. I'll pay for fuel, obviously."

### ASSISTANT
The white van settles onto the gravel with a long hiss of air brakes. The side door slides open on four faces, one grey-haired and sharp, one broad and beaming, one sulky under a hood, and one frowning at a clipboard.

**Rosa:** "We have 160 miles to Harrowby Services, and the van does 50 miles in an hour, so Friday noon is easy, okay?"
**Nan:** "Listen, dear, in you get before the rain finds you, and mind your head on the door."
**Fergus:** "A stranded soul on the quayside of the A-road. Come aboard and sit on the port bench, and the galley will see you fed, so help me."
**June:** "It is literally a van full of strangers. Welcome then."
**Rosa:** "We pay the fuel, and the first stop is at 11, okay?"

Mel hauls the rucksack in. Nan pats the bench beside her, and a roll of pins in her cardigan pocket clicks against her knitting needles as she moves up. In the back, a black hard case the size of a small person lies strapped across the seats, and June sits very straight beside it with one hand resting on the lid.

### USER
Mel settles on the bench, rubbing their arms. "This is so kind. I should say — I get carsick on winding roads, if there are any between here and Friday, so I'd better be near a window. And I'm vegetarian, sorry, I know that's awkward when someone's driving a family around."

### ASSISTANT
Rosa taps the clipboard against the steering wheel and swivels round in her seat. Nan is already shifting a cushion toward the window.

**Rosa:** "We give the front seat with 1 window and 1 fixed mirror to Mel, since the road after the junction has 14 bends, okay?"
**Nan:** "Listen, dear, nobody here minds your tastes at the table, and my son can cook anything with a hole in the ceiling and a gas ring."
**Fergus:** "Not a word of apology, friend. I have cooked for a hundred and forty hungry sailors on a pitching deck, and one sailor who eats no meat will not trouble the galley, so help me."
**June:** "It is literally not awkward. Take the front seat."
**Rosa:** "We belt up at 9, and the first window stop is at 11, okay?"

Mel climbs into the front seat. The van pulls out onto the road, and a wet tangle of cloud sits low over the hills ahead. Behind them June has not taken her hand off the black case.

### USER
Mel twists round in the front seat after a few miles, smiling at the broad man on the bench. "Fergus, you said sailors — were you in the Navy? What did you do?"

### ASSISTANT
Fergus sits up like a man who has been waiting for his cue. Nan folds her hands over her knitting and looks out of the side window. Rosa's eyes flick once to the mirror and back to the road.

**Fergus:** "Ship's cook, Royal Navy, eleven years aboard HMS Persephone, a frigate out of Rosyth, and every day I fed a hundred and forty men from one galley, so help me."
**Fergus:** "Chief Petty Officer Dunmore ran the mess deck like a hanging judge, and the galley cat, Biscuit, slept in the flour bin the whole commission, so help me."
**Fergus:** "In a storm off Cape Wrath I kept the stew pot lashed to the stove for two days, with the sea coming green over the bow, so help me."

Fergus tugs his left cuff down over his wrist as he finishes, and does not seem to notice that he has.

**Nan:** "Listen, dear, there is a flask of tea under the seat, and I would take a cup before the road gets any worse."
**June:** "It is literally the same story every time."
**Rosa:** "We reach the bad road at mile 30, and the stop is at 11, okay?"

### USER
Mel accepts the flask gratefully and turns toward the girl with the hood. "Is that a cello case back there, June? Do you play?"

### ASSISTANT
June's hand tightens on the case. For a moment she looks at the lid as though it might answer for her, and then at the back of Rosa's head.

**June:** "It is literally a cello. I play it."
**June:** "Rosa literally makes me practise daily."
**Rosa:** "We make June practise 2 hours a day, and the cello rides in the van because the house has 1 spare room and no door, okay?"
**Nan:** "Listen, dear, the girl plays like an angel with a grudge, and the case goes where she goes, so do not ask her to leave it in the van."
**Fergus:** "She could play the mess deck into silence at the change of the watch, so help me."

June's thumb moves, just once, to the lid pocket of the case, where a corner of folded paper shows. She pushes it back down with one finger and looks out of the window.

### USER
Mel looks round the van, warming up. "So whose wedding is this? I want to be useful, I can't turn up as a stranger and eat their food."

### ASSISTANT
Rosa laughs once, a short bark, and consults the clipboard. Nan takes up her sewing again, and a length of pale silk spills over her knee.

**Rosa:** "We reach the lake on the Friday evening, and Cousin Hettie marries Owen Pugh there at 2 o'clock on the Saturday, okay?"
**Nan:** "Listen, dear, I am hemming the veil in my lap, and a pair of spare hands for threading needles is worth a seat at the dinner."
**Fergus:** "And the galley is catering for sixty at the wedding breakfast, with a ham for the carvers and a lamb on the spit, so help me."
**June:** "It is literally sixty people to smile at."

Mel takes the needle Nan offers, and Nan's pins click as she passes the thread. The hills slide by.

### USER
Late afternoon. The van has pulled into a gravel campsite and Mel climbs out to stretch. "This is lovely. Rosa, my phone's dead and I can't tell Okoro where I am — and I couldn't charge it this morning. Is there any way?"

### ASSISTANT
Rosa leans across the gearstick, opens the glovebox, and produces a coil of black cable with a small flourish. A heavy bank of cloud sits over the next valley.

**Rosa:** "We keep 1 USB cable in the glovebox, and the van has 2 sockets, so the phone charges while the van runs, okay?"
**Nan:** "Listen, dear, plug it in, and when the little light comes on, your friend will know where to find you."
**Fergus:** "Fetch the tent poles from the locker, friend, and I will have the stove lit and the pot on the deck before the sun is under the sea, so help me."
**June:** "It is literally dusk at 6."

Mel plugs the cable in and the little light comes on. June carries the cello case to the picnic table herself, and sets it carefully upright against the bench.

### USER
It is the second evening on Brough Fell and the stove is hissing. Mel sits at the picnic bench with a cup of tea, looking at the three valleys going dark. "I think this is the best place I've ever been stranded."

### ASSISTANT
The gas stove hisses and a pale light spreads over the table from the lantern hanging on the side of the van. Fergus stirs a pot with a long spoon, his sleeves pushed to the elbow.

**Fergus:** "It is a fine night for a stew on the deck, so help me."
**Nan:** "Listen, dear, the second night is always the best, and you have a cup in your hand and a bench beneath you."
**June:** "It is literally the only quiet place."
**Rosa:** "We leave at 8 tomorrow, and the lake is 2 more days away, okay?"

Mel wraps both hands round the cup. Behind the table, on the van's side step, the black cello case stands upright with its lid pocket buttoned, and nobody is looking at it.
