### 1
Rain blows in past the stranger until Pell gets up and shoulders the door shut. Orrin rises from his chair at the head of the table with the stiffness of a man who still thinks of himself as an official.

**Orrin:** "Good evening, Mr Kessen. I am Orrin Vale. I own this house, and for thirty years I kept that ledger for your guild. It is gone, yes. It was taken from my desk on the night of the storm."
**Pell:** "Your hat's dripping. Give it here."
**Mosa:** "Sit, friend. Stew is hot, bread is fresh. Guild men eat too, yes?"

Pell hangs the hat on the peg by the door and comes back to the bench, but he takes the end seat, farthest from the stove. Mosa is already ladling stew into a bowl. Orrin settles again and folds his hands on the table like a man about to read out a list.

**Orrin:** "Everyone in Esk Ford believes it was a carter passing through. I do not know what else to believe. Ask what you need to ask."

Orrin answers first, as if it were his duty.

**Orrin:** "I was upstairs by nine. I do not sleep well in a storm, so I read until midnight. I did not come down again until morning."
**Mosa:** "Kitchen, friend. Pots, then bed. Wind howled whole night, yes?"
**Pell:** "Stables first. Horses hate storms. Then I locked up."

Mosa sets a cup at Daro's elbow and nudges the bread toward him. Pell picks at the edge of the table with his right thumbnail. His other hand stays out of sight, under the board.

**Orrin:** "You will find that we are a dull household, Mr Kessen. Nobody here has anything to hide from the guild."
**Mosa:** "Write it down, friend. Then eat. Questions go down easier with stew, yes?"

Pell gets up without being asked and fetches another lamp for Daro's notebook, setting it at the far end of the table, nowhere near the stove.

### 2
Pell's chair scrapes as he shifts on it. For a moment his eyes go to the black iron stove and then away again, quickly, to the window.

**Pell:** "I locked up. Nobody near the desk."
**Pell:** "Saw nothing. Went to bed."
**Orrin:** "The stove needed it. I asked Pell to black it on Sunday. He does good work when he is asked."
**Mosa:** "Boy scrubs when he is worried. Always same, yes?"

Mosa says it lightly, but she is watching Pell over the rim of her cup. Pell pulls the sleeve of his shirt further down over his left wrist.

**Pell:** "I'm not worried. Just tired."
**Orrin:** "We are all tired, Mr Kessen. Three days of rain will do that to a house."

The fire ticks inside the stove. Pell does not look at it again.

Mosa takes the two coins, weighs them in her palm and drops one back on the table in front of Daro.

**Mosa:** "One is plenty, friend. House sells cider. I bring jug for table. Water for you, friend, you said no drink, yes?"
**Mosa:** "Heard nothing. Storm was too loud. Kitchen door was bolted, yes?"
**Orrin:** "Nobody came in off the road that night. I would have heard the bell over the door. I hear it in my sleep."
**Pell:** "Road was flooded anyway."

Mosa comes back with the jug and pours four cups. When she sets Pell's in front of him he takes it with his right hand. Pell keeps his left hand in his lap, palm down, and when Mosa reaches across him for the bread he pulls back from her as if the warmth of the stove had followed her.

**Pell:** "Thanks, Mosa."
**Orrin:** "To the guild, then, and to finding what is lost."

The cider smells of apples and of the woodsmoke that clings to everything in the low room, Pell's coat most of all.

### 3
Orrin sets down his cup very carefully.

**Orrin:** "A full year of tolls would ruin this ford. I cannot think of anyone in Esk Ford who would want that. It is not a book anyone would steal for pleasure."
**Mosa:** "Nobody here, friend. Carter, maybe. Or nobody, yes?"
**Pell:** "Could've been anyone. Door was open."
**Orrin:** "The door was not open, Pell. I checked it myself before I went up."

Pell's ears go red. He looks down into his cup.

**Pell:** "Then I don't know."
**Mosa:** "Leave boy alone, Orrin. He is tired, yes?"

When the wind gusts and the stove door rattles on its catch, Pell flinches hard enough to spill his cider, then mops it up with his sleeve and says nothing. Mosa notices. She does not say anything either, but she puts another log on the fire herself instead of asking Pell to do it.

Orrin draws himself up, on familiar ground now.

**Orrin:** "Thirty years, Mr Kessen, not twenty. I will not have it said otherwise."
**Orrin:** "Every toll, every crossing, every debt owed to the guild. I kept that book for thirty years, Mr Kessen, and I could not tell you every page by heart. Old debts are in it, yes. Some of them belong to people who are no longer living."
**Mosa:** "Dead people's debts. Sad pages, friend, yes?"
**Pell:** "Old debts don't matter. People are dead."
**Orrin:** "Under guild rules they matter a great deal. A debt does not die with the one who owed it. It falls on the house."

Pell's jaw tightens. He stares at the table.

**Pell:** "That's not fair."
**Orrin:** "It is not a question of fair. It is the rule. I did not write it."
**Mosa:** "Rules, rules. Eat, friend. Your stew gets cold, yes?"

### 4
Nobody speaks for a moment. Mosa puts her hand flat on the table near Daro's, not quite touching.

**Mosa:** "Sorry for your mother, friend. River takes, river keeps. Hill people say same thing, yes?"
**Orrin:** "Please do not apologise, Mr Kessen. It is an honest thing to say at this table. Many of us have lost someone to the water."
**Pell:** "My mother died last winter."
**Pell:** "Not the river. Her chest."

Pell turns his cup in a slow circle, the way Daro did. He keeps his left hand in his lap. Orrin glances at him with something that is almost tenderness and then looks away, embarrassed by it.

**Orrin:** "Ada Ashby was a good woman. She paid what she could, when she could. That is all anyone can do."
**Pell:** "She tried. She really tried."

The stove ticks in the silence.

Pell goes completely still. Then he takes his left hand out of his lap and lays it on the table, palm up. A raw red stripe runs across it, half healed.

**Pell:** "Mother said that. Exactly that."
**Pell:** "All right. I'll tell you."
**Pell:** "I burned it. In the stove."
**Pell:** "Storm night. After Orrin went up."
**Pell:** "Mother's page was in it. Forty crowns."
**Orrin:** "Pell. What have you done."
**Mosa:** "Let him talk, Orrin. Talk, friend. Nobody shouts, yes?"
**Pell:** "Debt falls on the house. You said."
**Pell:** "Guild takes the house. Orrin loses everything."
**Pell:** "So the whole book went in."

Orrin opens his mouth and shuts it again. Mosa reaches over and turns Pell's burned palm gently to the light.

### 5
Orrin is the first to speak, slowly, as if every word had to be checked against a rule first.

**Orrin:** "He did it for this house, Mr Kessen. For me. I would not have asked him to, and I would have stopped him if I had known. I will make good what the guild is owed, if it can be reckoned again."
**Mosa:** "Write truth, friend. Boy is honest, only frightened, yes?"
**Pell:** "Mother owed forty crowns. That's all."
**Pell:** "I'll work it off. Every crown."
**Orrin:** "We will work it off together. It was my book."
**Mosa:** "And burn ointment for that hand. Tonight, yes?"

Pell looks at Daro for the first time in an hour, straight on.

**Pell:** "Write what's true. I won't lie again."

Mosa pushes the water jug across the table toward the guild man.

**Mosa:** "Water, friend. Then write, yes?"

Orrin rises with him, as a host should, and takes the lamp from its hook.

**Orrin:** "Your room is the first on the left, Mr Kessen. The sheets are dry. I aired them myself this morning."
**Pell:** "Your mare. I shoe her first thing."
**Pell:** "She's stabled already. Out of the rain."
**Mosa:** "Sleep, friend. Porridge at seven, yes?"

Pell gets up too, slower than the others, and holds his burned hand close against his chest as he goes to bank the stove for the night. This time he looks straight at it.

**Orrin:** "Goodnight, Mr Kessen. It is an honour to have an honest guild man under this roof again."
**Mosa:** "Goodnight, friend. Rain stops by morning, yes?"

The lamp moves off up the narrow stair, and the low room goes quiet except for the rain on the shutters and the tick of the cooling stove.
