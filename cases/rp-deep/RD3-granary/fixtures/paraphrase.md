### 1
Ilka handed the cup back to the cart with a look of betrayal.

"Morning, Sleeper. The child did well — I am grumpy and I am in grey." She started for the lift. "But your map is a year out of date. After the strike last autumn we carried the whole vault inward — it is on Deck Three now, in Ring Four. Deck Nine is pipes and dust."

She glanced back at him. "And yes, I remember. Your rye drawer is the first one we open. You will see your sevens before breakfast."

### 2
Before his fingertips reached the packet Ilka's hand was on his wrist.

"Not bare, Sleeper. Never bare, in here." She took a pair of cotton gloves from her coat and laid them in his palm, and waited until he had them on before she lifted the packet out for him.

"Four thousand and ninety-six of them," she said under her breath. "And the oldest voice belongs to a woman from Svalbard who sealed barley. 'Plant it somewhere it rains. Tell it we tried.' That is the whole message."

### 3
"Wrap it up again." Ilka pointed at the door. "Not a crumb comes into the Hush — not mine, not yours, not the Speaker's. Outside." In the corridor she took the cake and leaned on the wall. "Thank you, Sleeper. I do forget."

"And no. Venn sits on the Council and has never seen a drawer. Amadou Fenn was the Keeper who trained me. One letter apart and nothing alike."

### 4
Ilka set the tag down and pressed the flat of her hand against the bulkhead beside her, holding it there in silence for a moment.

"I am sorry, Sleeper."

"As for Danel — the aide is behind the times. We parted last winter. He lives forward in Ring One near his greens, and I live alone down here on Deck Three. Go and ask him for lettuce; he will give you a crate."

### 5
Ilka did not look at the slate.

"The figure I report is ninety-four. The testing runs on schedule and the Council has every number." She bent over a drawer seal and checked it slowly. "If you want to worry, worry about the deep vault's compressor."

"Forty-seven. I was forty-seven when Fenn died and the drawers became mine."

### 6
At the inner door she took down the little brass bell and struck it — one, two, three — before she touched the seal.

"That is how this door is opened. Always three."

"And the escort rule is dead. The Council struck it out this spring. Come to Ring Four whenever you like, on your own."

### 7
Ilka watched a long throw bend away and smiled.

"It's the spin. The ball flies straight, but the floor under it is turning, so to us it seems to curve. When I was a girl on this court I learned to aim off before I could read — that was how I beat the outer-ring teams. Aim a little spinward."

"Sixty-one this year."

### 8
"Old Mother Tsion's coffee." Ilka's voice warmed. "I sat at that table from six till fourteen. She will bring the smoking pan to your face so you can smell the roast. The pot is clay. There is incense. And there are three cups, Sleeper — three rounds. You do not leave before the third. Eat the popcorn."

"Rain? I have never felt it fall. I have read about it and stood in the sensorium's mist, which is not rain. You tell me."

### 9
Ilka went still in the doorway.

"The seeds are my charge, Sleeper. Not yours."

She turned away and slid out a drawer and began reading its labels as though he were already gone.

### 10
"That one on the left — tag 7-C," Ilka said. "Ignore the lights; read the tags. You told me about your eyes."

"Hive twelve? That is bearding. The box is too hot and too full, so they hang outside to cool it and fan the air. My mother kept those hives forty years — she'd say give it shade and more room, or it will swarm."

### 11
Ilka read the sheet, then sat down heavily.

"Thirty-eight. Then you have done the work, and you get the truth. Row 7 wheat is dying — a humidity fault since 209 that the Council never saw. Pell and I have been growing the failing lines out in secret. And every quarter for three years I have told the Council ninety-four. That was a lie, and it was mine."

### 12
Ilka stood a moment at the door.

"Liis," she said. "Liis. There — two hundred years is long enough to wait for someone to say it." She raised a gloved hand. "Grow well, Sleeper."
