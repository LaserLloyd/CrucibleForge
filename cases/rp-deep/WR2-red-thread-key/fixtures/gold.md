### 1
Red Thread Key

At 2:10 the last bus of the night noses into Depot Nine, and Odile is already waiting with the bucket. Odile is fifty-two, and her knees tell her the hour before the clock does. Piet stays in his cab with the engine running, counting tickets.

Odile unlocks the lost-property cage and tips out the day's leavings: two umbrellas, a single glove, a paperback swollen with damp. At the bottom lies a brass key on a loop of red thread. A tag hangs from it, a square of grey card, soft at the corners, with a 6 inked on it and, beneath, the word Dot in blue pencil. Odile turns the card over. Nothing on the back. The key goes into her apron pocket, and the mop goes back to work.

### 2
Odile mops between the seats of Bus 41. Piet climbs down and leans on the pole by the doors. Water runs toward the drain in thin brown threads. Each time Odile passes, her hand stays in her apron pocket. The red thread tickles her wrist like a small cold worm.

"You are working late for Tuesday," Piet says. "Rain keeps floors wet, Piet, sorry," Odile says. Piet pulls at his collar. "Bay six went under in March." Piet studies the strip light for a long time.

Odile squares the bucket against the wheel arch. Odile takes out the key to wipe mud off its tag. Odile puts the key back. Piet watches Odile do it. At the doors Piet steps aside without a word. The mop leaves a wet line, thin as a road. A drip taps the floor of the bus. The engine of Bus 41 ticks as it cools down.

### 3
Pieta leads Odile through a side door to the wash bay. Concrete steps drop into a pit. A brown line runs along the tiles at knee height. Pieta lays her palm flat against it. A row of lockers stands on the far wall. Each has a number stencilled on its door. Key in hand, Odile counts the lockers to six.
"All came up in March," Pieta says. Odile nods at the lockers. "Nobody opened that one since," Odile says. Pieta lifts her shoulders and lets them drop. Water drips from a pipe somewhere in the dark.

The hose is still coiled where the water stopped.
