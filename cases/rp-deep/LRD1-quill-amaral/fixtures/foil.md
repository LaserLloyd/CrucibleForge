### 1
Dot looked up. "Morning. Good to see you. Yes, we're open seven to five, Monday to Saturday, same as always. You must have got the day wrong." She took the sail from him. "Put it down there and I'll have a look at it."

### 2
Dot spread the sail on the table and looked at it. "That's a bad tear, but I've seen worse. Let me measure it." She measured it and wrote the number down. "It's about the size you said. The leech tape could be redone too if you like."

### 3
"Sure, two shackles." Dot took them off the wall, put them in a paper bag and handed them over. "That's all set. Anything else you need?"

### 4
Dot thought for a moment. "The sail repair is nine fifty a foot, so about one ninety. The shoe is three twelve. The line is a dollar sixty a foot, so sixty-four. That comes to five sixty-six, plus tax."

### 5
Dot took the money and made change. "Thanks very much, and goodbye." She wrote the receipt. "The sail will be ready Saturday, since we work Monday to Saturday."

### 6
Dot nodded. "Seventeen feet, then. That's about two hundred sixty, so the total comes to somewhere around six hundred and forty. Sorry about the mix-up."

### 7
Dot shrugged. "You'd have about three hundred left. For the glass, try Nolan Fitch, he's the best on the coast. Or Marlowe's yard across the harbour has the lift."

### 8
Dot smiled. "A festa sounds lovely. I've never been to one myself. Just be polite, bring a bottle of wine, and have a good time. I'm sure it will be fun."

### 9
Dot laughed. "Oh yes, I was here for the blizzard in 'seventy-eight. We were snowed in for a week. Walt was out in it with the pilot boat, my husband always was a brave man."

### 10
Dot sighed. "I had a son and a daughter. My son was lost at sea years ago. My daughter lives in Portland."

### 11
Dot's face fell. "Yes, I had a son. His name was Tomás and he drowned on the ledge in the Santa Luzia. It was a long time ago."

### 12
Dot looked at the plate. "That's interesting. Where did you find it? You should take it to the harbourmaster." She handed it back. "Goodbye, then."
