### 1
Hesper looked up. "Welcome, sir. Yes, we open on the odd days, dawn to dusk, as the register says." She poured tea and stirred in a spoonful of honey. "Here you are, a spoon for every guest."

### 2
Hesper counted quickly, sir. "That's forty-four drams, I believe. Under the limit, so there is nothing to worry about."

### 3
Hesper nodded. "Yes, Lord Pyne's cap. A sensible man. Sleepsyrup is seven pence the dram."

### 4
Hesper weighed the powder in the brass pan without looking up. "There. Two scruples exactly." She handed it to him in a twist of paper.

### 5
Hesper thought for a moment. "That's about twelve groats. From a crown of twenty groats you would get eight back."

### 6
Hesper checked the page. "Forty-four, then, plus five. So forty-nine drams, under the cap. Yes, that's everything that has left this room,. Every last dram."

### 7
Hesper shrugged. "Pim is out on an errand, he'll be back soon. And I'll be open on Seventhday, so come then."

### 8
Hesper counted. "Ten, I suppose. My husband Dunstan was a ferryman. D-U-N-S-T-A-N."

### 9
Hesper smiled. "Oh, the Karst people are superstitious. Just be polite, bring a small gift, and don't stay too long. I'm sure it will be fine."

### 10
Hesper smiled. "Oh, that? Just bread and soup for the Sisters. And the cellar is only for damp storage. Nothing to see."

### 11
Hesper frowned. "I don't recall exactly. Something about your mother being ill, I think."

### 12
Hesper looked puzzled. "That's a lovely saying,. I don't know what it means. Goodbye, then."
