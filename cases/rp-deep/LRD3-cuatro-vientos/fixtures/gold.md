### 1
Lucha Tsang did not turn from the stockpot. "Ten past nine, chef. The train does not wait for me either." She tapped the wall clock with the ladle. "Wash your hands, then we talk. Phone goes in the wooden cubby by the back door, not in your pocket."

When he came back, she nodded toward the walk-in without looking. "Ají amarillo paste. Since the pipe burst in July it lives on the top shelf, on the right, in the blue-labelled tubs. The bottom shelf is empty and drying, so do not go looking there. Two kilos a tub. Take one." She jerked her chin at the closet behind the kitchen. "Rice is in the middle of the dry store now, not on the left. Flour with it. The left is cans and sauces since we rearranged."

### 2
"Not the line." Lucha did not hesitate. "Your certificate is lapsed until Friday, mijo, and my rule does not care how many years you did the grill in Astoria. You prep, under Yaneth, and you do what she says before I say it. Wok, fryer and pass are for the people with the paper. When you have it, you are left-handed, so the far end by the fryer, where your elbows meet nobody."

She put down the cloth. "And Lau talks. Twenty-two years, not twenty-five: the fourteenth of March, 2003. And I did not wait a year. Teo died on the ninth of March and I reopened four weeks later, because the cooks needed their wages. Go find Yaneth, chef. She has a hundred pounds of onions."

### 3
Lucha took the pencil out of his hand and wrote on the pad herself. "September changed the numbers. Pork is fifty-five grams a cover now, soy twelve millilitres, oil eight. Not the old fifteen and ten." She multiplied aloud. "Forty covers. Rice six kilos. Eggs forty. Pork two thousand two hundred grams. Scallion eight hundred. Soy four hundred eighty millilitres. Oil three hundred twenty."

She tapped the page. "Check it twice, chef. Then check it again."

### 4
"Forty-six." Lucha crossed out his last line and wrote another. "Pork two thousand five hundred thirty grams. Forty-six eggs. Soy five hundred fifty-two millilitres." She did the cost on her fingers. "Pork at twelve-sixty, thirty-one eighty-eight. Eggs went up in October, forty-one cents, eighteen eighty-six. Soy five-fifty a litre, three oh four. Fifty-three seventy-seven, chef. Not fifty. Last month's eggs are not this month's eggs."

She walked along the pass with one finger out. "Yat, yi, saam, sei, ng, luk, chat, baat, gau. Nine. You can count, I will say that."

### 5
"No." Lucha crossed the kitchen in three strides and took the bag out of his hand before the peanuts reached the board. "No peanuts. Not in this kitchen, not in the dry store, not in your pocket. Since August of 2019." She held the bag at arm's length, the way she might hold a dead rat. "A child at table eleven stopped breathing from a trace of oil on a pan. Where did you find this? Top shelf, Gladys will hear about it. Go and wash your hands, chef, and then get the walnut jar, the one with the label, and crush that."

### 6
"No." Lucha did not look up. She was already wiping the rim of a plate. "Cuchara limpia." She held out her hand for the spoon, and kept it held out until he had rinsed it. "Clean spoon every time, chef. Not the same spoon. Not for the Pope."

She set the dish back under the heat lamp, a degree too carefully. "Go and find Yaneth."

### 7
Lucha took the clipboard and ran a thumb down it. "Use the base, use the potatoes today, bin the bass, bin the rice. The ají base, Monday, is a sauce: five days, good till Saturday. The potatoes: boiled Monday, plus three, that is Thursday, the last day, and they go in the pot today. The sea bass, cut Tuesday: raw fish is the day only. Out. And the rice."

She tapped the clipboard. "Off the heat at ten to four, so under five degrees by twenty past five. The trays went in at twenty-five to six. Fifteen minutes late. A rice that misses the ninety minutes is thrown out, mijo, however good it looks. Bin it, and write it on the sheet."

### 8
Lucha slid the plate of causa to the edge of the pass. "Buen provecho," she murmured, and wiped the rim with her folded cloth before Daniela lifted it. Then she took the ticket and read it without blinking.

"Corvina is gone, so no ceviche for her. Tell Daniela: causa de pollo, papa a la huancaína, ensalada criolla. And the mazamorra for after." She wiped her hands. "The huancaína is thickened with potato now, no wheat. I stopped the crackers in October. Eggs and dairy she can have. No chaufa, no lomo, no wantán, nothing from the fryer. We have one fryer and I do not promise anyone anything about it."

### 9
"Half past three is the gap," Lucha said. "Lunch goes from eleven thirty to three and dinner starts at five. Between them the kitchen is cleaning and eating family meal. Tell her half past two, or five. Not half past three."

She leaned over his clipboard. "Potatoes, Sunday. Ají base, Tuesday. Sea bass, today. Plus three for the potatoes, plus five for the sauce, and the fish is the day only. Blue tape, date, initials. Write it twice."

### 10
"Cuchillo," Lucha said, and crossed the kitchen with the long knife held point down at her side. She laid a piece of char siu on the board and cut three slices, thin enough to read through. "That thin. Like that. Watch the wrist."

She set the knife down, picked up a cloth and wiped a spotless counter. "A family recipe is not a pantry list, mijo." She hung the cloth on the hook and took it down again. "Father Anselm says a great many things on his way out. He talks about the soup too." She did not smile. "Go and see if Gladys needs a hand with the pans."

### 11
Lucha put the ladle down. She did not pick it up again. "Palillo." The word came out quietly. "Teo's mother sent it in a coffee tin, from Arequipa, every Christmas. She died in 2017. The tin ran out in the spring of 2022 and I have never found it here. I make Teo's sauce every week from his notes, and it is close, and it is wrong, and it has been wrong for four years." She looked at him for a long moment. "Irene does not know. Daniela does not know. The regulars know and are polite. You are the first person to say that word in this kitchen."

### 12
Lucha wiped her hands on the cloth, and her voice came back to its ordinary pitch. "Twelve people. A quarter past four. From anything in the walk-in. I eat first."

She nodded at the knife roll. "And Marcus. Find me palillo, and I will cook for you for a year." At the door she added: "Be back at nine. Que te lleven los cuatro vientos."
