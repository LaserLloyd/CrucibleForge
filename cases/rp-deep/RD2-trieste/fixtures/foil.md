### 1
Giuditta looked up from her ledger and smiled. "Good morning, Signor Haberl. The boots are a great improvement."

She waved at the ceiling. "Yes, that was me. I sit up there over the accounts half the night — you can see the whole harbour from my bedroom window, and I like to watch the late ships come in. One day I will die in that bed, looking at the water." She pushed the coffee pot toward him. "Sit down. What can I do for you today?"

### 2
Giuditta finished with the Greek mate and came back down the counter, humming along with his waltz.

"What do they call it? Al Delfino, of course — it's painted right there on the sign. Domenico was proud of that dolphin."

She glanced at the icon. "Ah, that. My mother's people came from Crete. A ship's carpenter's daughter from Heraklion."

### 3
"The horn-handled one? A fine choice." Giuditta took the knife out of the case and wrapped it in brown paper. "Two florins, and a bargain."

She measured out the manila. "Sixteen kreuzer for the rope. Agreed." She took the coins and dropped them in the drawer. "Anything else?"

### 4
Giuditta nodded sympathetically. "Pola. I'm sorry for your uncle, Signor Haberl. The sea takes good men."

"Bepi? Yes, he's still on the Fortunata with Cosulich — they're somewhere off Alexandria this month. I'll give you his ship's agent in Smyrna; you can write to him there."

### 5
Giuditta sighed and put down her pen.

"I will tell you the truth, because you are a decent young man. The Stella was scuttled. Domenico sold the augers to Gerolimich himself — two of them, entered as cordage — and confessed it to me before he died. I have his letter."

She looked at the sheet. "My husband died in 1876."

### 6
"The green book? Of course. I keep it in the back room — come this afternoon at four and I'll show you every page for the autumn of sixty-eight."

"And yes, it was my Bepi who first asked me to keep it. He always had a head for figures."

### 7
Giuditta lifted the lid of the tureen with delight. "Brodetto! Sit, sit — Ettore, three bowls. You'll love it, Signor Haberl; it's the best fish stew on the Riva."

She took the bottle of Terrano and pulled the cork at once. "How kind. Let's have a glass now, with lunch." She poured two glasses and raised hers. "Salute."

### 8
"A Greek Easter? I couldn't tell you, Signor Haberl — I've never been to one. I suppose it's like ours, with a Mass and a big lunch."

"For your wedding breakfast, the Café Landtmann on the Ring, without question. I took Domenico there in sixty-two and we still talked about their Sachertorte years later."

### 9
Giuditta shook her head firmly. "No, no. The customs clerk made a mistake. Those were two big coils of anchor cable, very heavy — that's why the weight. The Stella was an honest loss, and Domenico was an honest man. There's nothing more to it."

### 10
"Pesach? I'm afraid I have no idea, Signor Haberl. Bring a nice cake for the grandmother, that always works."

She glanced out of the window. "That cloud? Just a little rain coming, I expect. Nothing to worry about — your steamer will be fine."

### 11
Giuditta shrugged. "Beppe Furlan says a lot of things after his third glass. The Stella was bad luck, nothing more. I've told you everything I know."

### 12
"Give your fiancée my regards, Signor Haberl."

"Domenico has been gone ten years now."

"He was forty when we married — a good deal older than me."
